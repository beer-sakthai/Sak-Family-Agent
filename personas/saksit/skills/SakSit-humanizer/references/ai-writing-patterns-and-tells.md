# AI Writing Patterns, Tells & De-Slop Catalog

This reference documents the 29 telltale signs of AI-generated text based on the WikiProject AI Cleanup guidelines and practical editorial heuristics.

---

## 1. Top Telltale Vocabulary Words (The "AI Vocabulary")

Replace or eliminate these dead giveaways:
- `delve` / `delving` $\to$ explore, examine, look into, dig into
- `testament` ("a testament to...") $\to$ shows, proves, demonstrates
- `tapestry` ("a rich tapestry of...") $\to$ mix, collection, range
- `beacon` ("a beacon of hope...") $\to$ leader, example, guide
- `crucial` / `pivotal` / `paramount` $\to$ key, main, important
- `foster` / `fostering` $\to$ build, encourage, help, create
- `interplay` / `dance` $\to$ relationship, connection
- `unwavering` / `relentless` $\to$ steady, consistent
- `multifaceted` $\to$ complex, broad
- `spearhead` / `spearheading` $\to$ lead, run, direct

---

## 2. Structural & Rhetorical AI-isms

1. **The Puffed-Up Intro / Summary Echo**: Starting with grand generic statements ("In today's fast-paced digital world..."). *Fix: Cut the first paragraph; start directly with the core event or fact.*
2. **Superficial "Rule of Three"**: Constantly grouping clauses in triads ("innovation, collaboration, and excellence"). *Fix: Use 1 or 2 concrete nouns; avoid artificial groupings.*
3. **The Preachy Wrap-Up**: Ending with a patronizing rhetorical summary ("Ultimately, only time will tell..."). *Fix: Delete the conclusion entirely or end on a concrete next step.*
4. **False Equivalence / False Balance**: Over-hedging every opinion with "While some argue X, others maintain Y." *Fix: Take a stance or provide specific cited positions.*
5. **Bold Word Overuse in Lists**: Bolding the first two words of every bullet point uniformly. *Fix: Use natural sentence structure without uniform formatting templates.*

---

## 3. Injecting Soul & Authentic Voice

- **Vary sentence lengths**: Follow a long, descriptive sentence with a 3-word sentence.
- **Use domain-specific colloquialisms**: Use the team's real vernacular (e.g., "shipped", "broke in prod", "hotfix").
- **Ground in concrete details**: Mention specific file paths, numbers, or dates rather than vague abstractions ("significant performance improvements").
