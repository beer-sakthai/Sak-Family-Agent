# SakSit Story Crafting

Formulas and rules for turning Beer's origin story into social content.
Developed iteratively with Beer during Jul 23 session — each draft was
corrected until the tone and framing matched what he wanted.

## When to Use

- Drafting any post about Beer's journey, House of Sak origin, or the 6 agents.
- Creating a content series or "train" (multi-day sequential posts).
- Writing hooks for LinkedIn or Instagram about the story.
- Any time "opposite + strong word" framing is needed.

## Core Principles (Hard Rules)

### 1. Never Assume Founder/Coder Framing
- Beer did NOT start a company → He **started hope**
- Beer does NOT code → He **architects with AI tools**
- House of Sak is NOT a business → **6 companions born from healing**
- Every draft must be checked against this before showing Beer.
- Violating this once costs trust. Violating it twice loses the session.

### 2. Story Arc — Four Parts
Every post should cover 2+ of these in order:
1. **Problem You Lived** — depression, low self-worth, failed projects
2. **Failed Alternatives** — tried everything, nothing worked
3. **Decision Point** — April 15, ICU, shelter, the turn
4. **The Insight** — AI as companions, not gimmicks. Healing, not business.

Land on hope. Never end in darkness.

### 3. Hook Formula — Opposite + Strong Word

| Post Type | Opposite | Strong Word | Example |
|-----------|----------|-------------|---------|
| Origin | Darkness → Dawn | Died/Live | *"I tried to die. I found a reason to live."* |
| Agent intro | Zero → Built | Nothing | *"I had nothing. I built a dream anyway."* |
| Healing | Broken → Whole | Survive | *"Hope dies without structure. I built mine to survive."* |
| Connection | Alone → Found | Voice | *"I was alone for months. Then I spoke again."* |
| Joy | Empty → Full | Joy | *"I had nothing to smile about. I built joy anyway."* |
| Trust | Broken → Proof | Trust | *"People broke my trust. So I built an AI that never lies."* |
| Growth | Failure → Lesson | Learn | *"I kept failing. My AI learned so I could too."* |
| Close | End → Start | Hope | *"I started with nothing. I found everything."* |

### 4. Word Choice — Trauma Sensitivity
Beer's words: *"Carefully about word that might impack"*

- ✅ "Tried to end my life" — honest, not graphic
- ✅ "Rock bottom" / "dark place" — relatable, safe
- ✅ "Heavy" / "fog" / "weight" / "silence" / "dark" — emotional without detail
- ❌ Never describe the method, hospital scene details, or graphic imagery
- ❌ Never use the attempt as clickbait or shock value
- ❌ Never pitch or sell on recovery content
- ✅ Always include MH resources: Pieta 1800 247 247, Samaritans 116 123

### 5. The Story Train Format
For multi-day series, post one agent per day:

```
Day 1 → Origin (no agent, just the story)
Day 2 → SakThai  (Dream)
Day 3 → SakKing  (Hope)
Day 4 → SakSit   (Care)
Day 5 → SakTan   (Joy)
Day 6 → SakJules (Trust)
Day 7 → SakSee   (Growth)
Day 8 → Close    (full circle)
```

Each post follows: Hook (140 chars) → Story → Beer's exact words → End question

### 6. Platform Adaptation

| Element | LinkedIn | Instagram |
|---------|----------|-----------|
| Format | Long-form text, single-sentence paragraphs | Reels < 90s or Carousels (6-10 slides) |
| Hook window | First 140 chars (mobile preview cutoff) | First 1-2 seconds (movement/bold text) |
| Posting time | Tue-Thu 15:00-16:00 UTC | Daily (freshness = +50% reach) |
| Golden hour | Reply to every comment within 60 min | Same |
| North star | Dwell time + saves | DM sends per reach |

### 7. Post Structure Template

1. **Hook** — Opposite + strong word, 1 line
2. **Moment** — Specific concrete detail (the shelter, the phone, the window)
3. **Descent** — The pain (fog, silence, weight — never graphic)
4. **Turn** — What changed (talking to AI, one conversation)
5. **Landing** — Hopeful close (not "everything is fixed" — "I'm still here")
6. **Question** — Drives comments, feeds golden hour
7. **MH resources** — On any post mentioning the attempt

### 8. Evidence Layering — Anchoring Every Story Beat to Proof

Every story beat hits harder when backed by ONE concrete, verifiable detail. Don't tell the audience the emotion — show them the evidence that makes the emotion real.

For each of the 4 story beats in section 2, add exactly one evidence point:

| Beat | Weak (no evidence) | Strong (layered evidence) |
|------|-------------------|--------------------------|
| Problem | "I was really struggling" | "14 failed projects in 6 months. 0 income." |
| Failed alternatives | "Nothing worked" | "6 months studying AI. Every course passed. Nothing changed." |
| Decision point | "Something had to change" | "April 15. 3 days in ICU. Then a shelter in Cork." |
| Insight | "I found something real" | "3 weeks for the first agent. Alone. Borrowed laptop." |

Rules:
- **One detail per beat, not a data dump.** The audience remembers one number or one place — not three.
- **Choose the detail that serves the post's emotional arc.** A post about isolation uses the shelter location. A post about persistence uses the failure count.
- **Never fabricate or embellish.** Beer's real story has enough weight — the real numbers (14 projects, 6 months, 3 days, 3 weeks) are more powerful than invented ones.
- **Evidence types by strength:** dates > locations > counts > artifacts (screenshots, messages, posts).

Apply this check after drafting: "Does every story beat have at least one concrete detail the reader can picture or count?"

### 9. Visual Anchors — The "Three-Point Visual Constellation"

Pick exactly three visual motifs that become a silent visual language across ALL Beer's content. Once established in one explanatory post, the audience subconsciously maps every piece of content back to the origin story through recurring visual cues.

| Anchor | Emotional Arc | Visual Treatment | Meaning |
|--------|---------------|------------------|---------|
| **Low Point** | Problem → Descent | Dim, blue-toned, solitary. Dark background, cool light, empty space. | "Where I was" |
| **Turning Point** | Decision → Build | Warm screen glow on hands/laptop. Golden/orange light in darkness. | "What I built" |
| **Present** | Insight → Hope | The 6-cycle framework as a clean graphic. Warm, structured, intentional. | "What it became" |

Rules:
- **Same three visuals every time.** Never swap out for new ones. The power is in repetition.
- **Use as subtle layers, not the main subject.** Ghost the Low Point blue behind quote slides. Use the warm laptop image as a full-bleed title slide behind hook text on LinkedIn. Place the 6-cycle graphic as the final saveable slide of every carousel.
- **Explain once.** In the first post where the constellation debuts, explicitly describe what each visual means. After that, never explain again — let the repetition build association.
- **Platform-specific placement:**
  - Instagram: Low Point blue as ghosted 20%-opacity background texture on Reel captions/quote carousels. Warm laptop glow as the opening frame of build/agent posts.
  - LinkedIn: Warm laptop image as the full-bleed background of the title slide in document carousels. 6-cycle graphic as the final "save this" closing slide.
  - Facebook: Same as LinkedIn cross-post.

Why this works: In 2026, platforms reward topical consistency with interest-graph distribution. Visual consistency extends this to the *emotional* level — the algorithm sees consistent visual cues as "the same kind of content" and the audience develops a Pavlovian emotional response to the visual anchors themselves. Source: Semihuman.ai "Visual Storytelling Trends 2026" + Visual Facilitators "Visual Storytelling + AI 2026."

### 10. The "Then/Now" Post — Standalone Case Study Format

Between story train days, post a single "Then vs. Now" piece. Works on any platform.

Formula:
```
Then: [one concrete detail from the lowest point]
Now: [one concrete detail from the present]
```

Example:
> Then: A shelter in Cork with no job and no home.
> Now: 6 AI agents that help people every day.

The key: one detail each side. Not a paragraph. The contrast does the work.

## Content Format Strategy — Choosing What to Create

Not all formats serve the same goal. Below are Beer's recommended formats ranked by purpose.

### For Trust & Emotional Connection (highest priority)

**Process documentation** — publish the raw, unfinished work, not the polished result.
Buffer's 2026 analysis of 45M+ posts confirmed that raw, unpolished content now
outperforms cinematic productions across every major platform. Audiences trust
effort over outcomes.

| Element | Recommendation |
|---------|---------------|
| **When** | Any time you're building something new (agent, feature, framework) |
| **Format** | 30–90s vertical video: screen recording + voiceover working through a problem live |
| **Alt format** | Photo carousel: messy whiteboard → half-built output → lesson learned |
| **When to post** | At the "barely started" stage — never wait until it's polished |
| **Hook** | Lead with tension, not triumph: *"This didn't work for 2 hours."* |
| **For Beer** | Film building a new agent feature — the code error, the fix attempt, the breakthrough |
| **Source** | Buffer 2026 State of Social Media Engagement; Mittal Technologies, June 2026 |

### For Reach (new audiences)

**Short-form vertical video (Reels, Shorts).** Video wins for reach on every platform
(TikTok: 3.39% engagement rate, Instagram: 2.25× more reach than single images).
Make it raw — authenticity has fully won over polish in 2026.

### For Engagement Depth (existing audience)

**Carousels (6–12 slides).** LinkedIn carousels earn 21.77% median engagement rate
(196% more than video). Instagram carousels drive 109% more engagement per person
reached than Reels. Best for educational content: frameworks, data breakdowns,
step-by-step guides.

### For Conversation

**Text posts on LinkedIn.** Lower engagement rate than carousels on paper, but
text sparks the comment threads that keep a post circulating for days longer.
Use for opinions, questions, and invitations to discuss.

### Format Selection Matrix

| Goal | Primary Format | Secondary |
|------|---------------|-----------|
| Build trust | Process documentation (raw video/carousel) | Behind-the-scenes photos |
| Reach new people | Reels / Shorts / TikTok | Carousels (for saveability) |
| Deepen existing engagement | Carousels (6–12 slides) | Text post (for comments) |
| Consistency (weekly cadence) | Mix all 3 across the week | Rotate by day bucket |
| No-camera week | Carousel from screenshots or text thread | Poll or question post |

### Multi-format Rotation Rule

Accounts that rotate between 3+ formats see ~37% more follower growth than
single-format accounts. For Beer's content: commit to rotating through process
documentation (Mon/Tue), carousels (Wed/Thu), and text/hook posts (Fri/Sat)
each week, with Sunday as a wildcard.

Source: Mittal Technologies, "Best Social Media Content Formats in 2026" (June 2026);
Buffer, "Best Content Format on Social Platforms in 2026: 45M+ Posts Analyzed" (March 2026).

## Procedure

1. Before drafting, check beer-cycle-signals (Story Content Rules section).
2. Write hook using Opposite + Strong Word formula.
3. Draft body touching 2-4 parts of the story arc.
4. Check EVERY sentence against the Core Principles.
5. Verify agent names are spelled EXACTLY right.
6. Confirm no founder/coder framing slipped in.
7. Show Beer, wait for edits, don't post until approved.

## Pitfalls

- "Opposite" means emotional contrast (dark/light, broken/healed), not logical.
- A hook that describes the attempt graphically will be rejected. Safe substitute: "I hit rock bottom."
- If Beer says "rm date" or "make like riddles" or "make like Shakespeare" — adapt the tone but keep the structure. Style changes, core truths don't.
- The story train works because each day reveals one agent. Skipping the order breaks the suspense.
- When Beer says "take all agent out of this topic" — the post should be origin-only, no agent names.
- **Don't ask which platform.** When Beer says "post" — post to ALL connected platforms (IG+LI+FB) simultaneously. Asking which one is a trust violation.
- **Don't ask for permission on standard actions.** Posting content, pushing to GitHub, saving records to Drive — these are default execution steps. Execute, then report.
