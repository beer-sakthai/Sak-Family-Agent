## Key Findings — 2026 Algorithm Changes

### 1. Semantic Meaning (New Primary Signal)
LinkedIn's algorithm now reads for the *actual substance* of posts, not just hashtags or keywords. It's "learning to understand the actual substance of your posts, not just the hashtags you attach to them."

### 2. Topic Consistency = Authority (NEW in 2026)
**Key quote:** "Consistency builds authority. When you stick to two or three topics, the algorithm learns your expertise and surfaces your content to the right people."

This is the most significant change. Posting across many topics dilutes the algorithm's ability to categorise you. Sticking to 2-3 consistent topics trains the algorithm to recognise you as a domain expert, giving preferential distribution.

### 3. Relevance Beats Recency
"A useful LinkedIn post can keep showing up days or even weeks later if it matches someone's interests." This is a shift from the previous model where recency was a strong signal. Now evergreen, topic-anchored content has much longer shelf life.

### 4. Video Growth Accelerating
"LinkedIn reported its third straight quarter of double-digit growth in video uploads in Q1 2026." Video is a growing priority format on the platform.

### 5. Spam Filter Strengthened — Explicit Penalties
LinkedIn now explicitly calls out these as spam-filter triggers:
- **Tagging people unrelated to your post** to chase reach
- **"Comment YES"** artificial engagement tactics
- **Hashtag stuffing** with irrelevant tags
- **Low-quality, poorly written, or unprofessional content**

### 6. Not Designed for Virality
LinkedIn specifically says it "is not designed for virality." It's built for sharing knowledge and updates with people who can use that information to learn and advance their careers.
