# Same-Day Delta Detection: sakthai-plus-1.5b (2026-07-30)

## What happened

Two health checks ran on the same day for `Nanthasit/sakthai-plus-1.5b`:
- First eval: ~20:56 UTC (by an earlier cron run)
- Second eval: ~21:11+ UTC (this session)

Despite the model being <12 hours old with 0 downloads and 0 likes, the delta
detected meaningful changes:

| Field | Before | After | Signal |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| `lastModified` | 2026-07-30T20:56:49Z | 2026-07-30T21:11:10Z | +14 min |
| `sha` | `0306cd2c6319` | `35af2ee63156` | Post-publish commit |
| Author rank | 15 of 18 | 13 of 18 | New sibling models published |

## Why this matters

Same-day deltas catch metadata-only commits (model card fixes, tag additions,
benchmark placeholder updates) that popularity metrics miss. They prove the
model is actively maintained even at zero traction.

## Implementation pattern

In `eval_metadata.changes_since_previous`, always compare:
1. `downloads` and `likes` (exact match to previous YAML)
2. `lastModified` (timestamp from single-model API response)
3. `sha` (commit hash — note SHA will change after YAML upload too)
4. Author rank (can shift even without target model changes)

## Key pitfalls

- **SHA changes on every upload**: The verification script uploads a new YAML
  file, creating a new commit. The `sha` field will NEVER match between
  pre-upload eval time and post-upload verification. Skip SHA in verification.
- **`createdAt` may be missing**: Fresh models may not have `createdAt` in
  `/api/models/{id}` response. Use `lastModified` as fallback.
