## Trending Model: openbmb/UltraX-0.6B-Preview

**The problem UltraX solves:** As web-scale pretraining data hits diminishing returns under standard Scaling Laws, the bottleneck has shifted from *how much* data to *how good* the data is. Existing refinement approaches fall into two inadequate camps: rule-based filters (cheap but rigid — can't handle instance-level noise like concatenated news feeds or SEO spam) and LLM-based rewriting (high quality but too slow and unreliable for billions of documents). UltraX bridges this gap with a 0.6B-parameter model that predicts structured editing function calls instead of generating free-form rewrites.

**Architecture & approach:** Built on a causal language model (0.6B params, 20K context window, trained via full-parameter SFT with ms-swift + DeepSpeed ZeRO3), UltraX takes web-crawled text with line-number markers and predicts a sequence of **five atomic operations**: `keep_all()`, `remove_all()`, `remove_lines(start, end)`, `replace_str(line, old, new)`, and `add_line(base, sub_idx, content)`. The critical innovation is the LAM + DCR pipeline — Line Alignment and Mapping aligns raw/refined text pairs at line level, and Dynamic Context Replacement converts character-level differences into reliable `replace_str` calls anchored to unique context. A sliding-window prediction with global operation aggregation stabilizes inference across long documents, and the model outputs are validated through systematic post-processing before deterministic execution.

**Benchmarks & results:** On FineWeb (20B tokens, trained into a 1B MiniCPM, evaluated zero-shot across 10 benchmarks), UltraX achieved an average of **46.14** — significantly outperforming both the raw corpus (45.08) and the prior state-of-the-art ProX-C (45.05), and winning on all 10 benchmarks. The instruction-guided variant with edit-weighted sampling delivered the best results; the no-instruction ablation still beat raw data (45.73), proving programmatic refinement alone brings gains. UltraX also matched or exceeded baselines with **fewer training tokens** — at the 16B-token checkpoint, it already reached 45.5 vs raw's 44.3 at 20B. In human evaluation, UltraX scored 9.60/10 vs ProX-C's 9.17, with dramatically lower variance (0.85 vs 1.57) and near-zero noise edits (0.38% vs 2.59%). Qualitative case studies show UltraX correctly preserves product specifications, restores concatenated form fields via multiple `add_line` calls, and detects gibberish/SEO spam — where ProX-C either kept the junk or deleted valuable content.
