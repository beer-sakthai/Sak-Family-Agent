## Deep Dive: `thinkingmachines/Inkling`

**Trending score: 716 · 1,487 likes · Released July 14, 2026 · Apache 2.0**

Inkling is Thinking Machines' first open-weight frontier multimodal model — a **975B-parameter sparse MoE with 41B active parameters**, making it one of the largest publicly available open-weight models on the Hub. Its architecture is a 66-layer decoder-only transformer with a novel MoE backbone: **256 feed-forward experts with top-6 routing plus 2 always-active shared experts**, totaling 258 expert slots per token. Attention layers alternate between local and global patterns, and the model natively processes text, images (via a hierarchical patch encoder with 40px patches and 2-frame temporal patching), and audio (via discrete mel-token encoding at 16kHz WAV). The entire hidden dimension is 6,144 across all modalities — text, vision, and audio all project into the same shared space for joint decoding by the transformer.

**Benchmarks tell a compelling story for a model of this active-parameter count.** On AIME 2026, Inkling scores **97.1%** — competitive with closed-weight frontiers (DeepSeek V4 Pro: 96.7%, Gemini 3.1 Pro: 98.3%). It achieves **77.6% on SWE-Bench Verified** (vs. Kimi K2.5's 76.8% and Nemotron 3 Ultra's 70.7%) and an impressive **74.1% on MCP Atlas** (agentic tool-use benchmark), trailing only Claude Fable 5 and GPT 5.6 Sol among the comparison set. Vision benchmarks show solid capability: **73.5% MMMU Pro (Standard 10)** and **78.1% Charxiv RQ**. The audio pipeline is also benchmarked — **77.2% MMAU** and **91.4% VoiceBench** — making Inkling a genuinely tri-modal (text + image + audio) offering. Safety evaluations are thorough: **98.6% StrongREJECT** (refusal of harmful requests) and **78.0% FORTRESS Adversarial** (jailbreak resistance), with benign instruction following at 95.9%.

**Getting started is well-supported.** The model ships with native integrations for SGLang, vLLM, TokenSpeed, Unsloth, and Hugging Face Transformers (PRs already merged). A Tinker Playground provides hosted demos, and there's an NVFP4 quantized variant (`thinkingmachines/Inkling-NVFP4`) for reduced memory footprint. The 108 safetensors shards (BF16) represent the full 975B parameter count — so practical local use will want the NVFP4 quant or 4-bit via Unsloth. The Hugging Face blog post at `hf.co/blog/thinkingmachines-inkling` has a detailed architecture breakdown, and the Tinker Cookbook (`github.com/thinking-machines-lab/tinker-cookbook`) provides complete inference recipes. For vLLM serving: `recipes.vllm.ai/thinkingmachines/Inkling`.
