**Space Spotlight: `microsoft/TRELLIS`** — Scalable 3D Generation from Images

TRELLIS (Structured 3D Latents for Scalable and Versatile 3D Generation) is Microsoft's flagship 3D generative model Space, racking up 4,775 likes and running on Gradio with ZeroGPU acceleration. It turns a single input image (or multiple views) into a full 3D asset in under a minute. The secret sauce is **SLAT** (Structured LATent) — a unified 3D representation that sits on a sparsely-populated 3D grid, infused with dense multiview visual features from a powerful vision foundation model. This lets TRELLIS decode into *three* different output formats from the same latent: Radiance Fields, 3D Gaussians, and textured meshes (exportable as GLB). The Space ships with model sizes from 666M to 2.4B parameters, all trained on 500K diverse 3D objects.

**Technical highlights.** The pipeline runs in two stages: (1) **sparse structure generation** (rectified flow transformer sampling the coarse 3D layout at 12 steps, CFG 7.5) followed by (2) **structured latent generation** (refining appearance/texture at 12 steps, CFG 3.0). Gradio's `@spaces.GPU` decorator powers both the core generation and a separate GLB extraction step (simplify + texture bake at up to 2048px). The UI offers seed control, guidance strengths, and a multi-image mode using either stochastic or multidiffusion algorithms. Output previews render 120-frame MP4 videos showing both the Gaussian splat and normal-mapped mesh side by side. Since the official Space restricts example-only images, third-party forks have popped up with full upload support — a sign of healthy community adoption.
