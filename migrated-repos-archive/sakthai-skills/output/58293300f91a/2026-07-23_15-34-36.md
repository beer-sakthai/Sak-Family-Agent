## Space of the Day: KlingTeam/LivePortrait

Face animation has been a fast-moving frontier, and **KlingTeam/LivePortrait** (3,767 likes, Gradio) is one of the most polished implementations on the Hub. At its core, it's an image-to-video pipeline that takes a single portrait photo and a driving video, then transfers the driving video's facial motion (head pose, eye blinks, lip movements) onto the static portrait — producing a fully animated video where the person in the photo speaks and moves exactly like the driver. It also supports **retargeting** — you can independently control eye-open ratio and lip-open ratio on a portrait via sliders, without needing a driving video at all.

Architecturally, LivePortrait uses a modular system of five trained components: an appearance feature extractor (F), a motion extractor (M) built on **ConvNeXtV2-Tiny** that predicts 21 keypoints, a warping module (W) with occlusion estimation, a SPADE-based generator (G) that upsamples from 256→512 px, and a stitching/retargeting module (S) that refines the motion field. The pipeline uses **ONNX Runtime GPU** for inference and runs on HF ZeroGPU (`@spaces.GPU(duration=240)`) — a smart choice that keeps it accessible without paid compute. The app processes both video-driven animation (yielding side-by-side and generated-only outputs) and single-image retargeting, all through a clean Gradio Blocks interface with accordion layouts, example galleries, and validation (e.g., square-video checks).

What makes LivePortrait stand out is how it handles the motion transfer problem without requiring a source video — just one photo and a driving clip. The SPADE generator's 2× upscale produces 512×512 output from 256×256 processing, and the paste-back feature lets the animated face be composited back onto the original uncropped photo. It's the kind of Space that demonstrates how far face reenactment has come: near-real-time, controllable, and running on free-tier GPU infrastructure.
