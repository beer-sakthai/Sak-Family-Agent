## ISO: An RLVR-Native Optimization Stack

**arXiv 2607.19331** · Jul 21 2026 · Authors: Hanqing Zhu, Wenyan Cong, Zhizhou Sha, Sagnik Mukherjee, Xinyuan Song, David González-Martínez, Xiaoxia Wu, Yuandong Tian, Shiwei Liu, David Z. Pan, Zhangyang "Atlas" Wang

RL with verifiable rewards (RLVR) — the paradigm behind DeepSeek-R1, GRPO, and most of the current reasoning-model boom — has a blind spot. Everyone optimizes the reward function, the rollout strategy, and the data pipeline. But the actual optimization layer (how reward feedback becomes weight updates) is just inherited wholesale from pre-training: AdamW on the full parameter space, no questions asked. This paper asks whether that inheritance is optimal, and the answer is a clear **no**.

The authors study the singular-value decomposition of model weights before and after RLVR fine-tuning. They discover **spectral inheritance**: RLVR training largely preserves the base model's singular-value spectrum (the "importance magnitudes" of each weight direction) while rewriting the associated input and output singular frames (the directions themselves). In other words, RLVR doesn't need to re-learn what matters — it just reorients what already matters toward the reward signal. This observation leads to **Isospectral Optimization (ISO)**, a framework that freezes the spectrum and only optimizes the frames.

ISO comes in two flavours. **ISO-Merger** (offline) takes multiple specialist models fine-tuned from the same base, extracts their frame changes, and merges them back onto the frozen base spectrum — no data, no rollouts, no distillation, and it beats all compared data-free merging methods on aggregate performance. **ISO-Optimizer** (online) is a drop-in replacement for AdamW/Muon that constrains updates to the frame subspace. On Qwen3-8B-Base, vanilla AdamW hits 0.495 aggregate accuracy after 270 training steps. ISO-AdamW matches it in **100 steps** (2.7× faster) and pushes to 0.509 in 210. The pattern holds across model scales from 1.5B to 8B and across reasoning and coding tasks.

Why this matters: RLVR post-training is becoming the dominant paradigm for LLM capability injection, yet its optimization layer has been cargo-culted from pre-training. ISO provides the first principled, empirically grounded alternative — one that respects the actual structure of reward-driven adaptation. The 2-3× step reduction means cheaper post-training runs, and the clean merging strategy means combining specialist capabilities without costly multi-task data mixing. For a household running on zero-cost infrastructure, anything that makes post-training more sample-efficient is directly relevant — and ISO's core insight (freeze the spectrum, rotate the frames) is elegant enough to influence optimizer design across the field.
