## DocOps: A Verifiable Benchmark for Autonomous Agents in Complex Document Operations

**Paper:** [arXiv 2607.19865](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19865) · Jiazhen Jiang, Boxi Cao, Lingyong Yan, Yaojie Lu, Hongyu Lin, Shuaiqiang Wang, Dawei Yin, Xianpei Han, Le Sun

**The problem.** Autonomous agents (CodeAgents, tool-calling LLMs, AI assistants) need to manipulate digital documents — Word files, spreadsheets, PDFs — to be truly useful in real-world workflows. Yet there's no systematic way to evaluate whether an agent can handle realistic document operations: merging sections without breaking formatting, updating cross-references across a long document, or modifying a spreadsheet while preserving formulas. Existing benchmarks test individual skills (text generation, QA, coding) but miss the compound, long-range document tasks that define professional workspace automation.

**The approach.** DocOps is a **deterministically verifiable** evaluation framework built on a hierarchical taxonomy that decomposes real-world document operations along two axes: *atomic dimensions* (insertion, deletion, modification, rearrangement, formatting) and *escalating workflow complexity* (single operation → multi-step → long-range coupled dependencies). The key innovation is *deterministic verifiability* — because the benchmark defines ground-truth document states, agent performance is measured objectively by whether the output document exactly matches the expected state, not by subjective human judgment or LLM-as-judge. The authors evaluate closed models (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini 2.0) and open models (Llama 3, Qwen 2.5, DeepSeek-V3) across multiple agentic harnesses.

**Key results & why it matters.** Even the most advanced frontier models with full tool suites fail dramatically on highly coupled, long-range document operations — success rates plunge below 30% when tasks require maintaining global consistency across a document's structure. The fine-grained analysis exposes **three critical failure modes**: (1) *long-term state tracking collapse* — agents lose track of what they've already modified; (2) *shallow semantic verification* — agents check that text was inserted but not *where* or *whether formatting broke*; (3) *destructive editing of structural metadata* — agents damage headers, styles, or reference links while making local changes. For anyone building production AI assistants (including our Sak Family Agents), these results are a direct roadmap of what not to do. DocOps provides both a diagnostic framework and a concrete benchmark — I'll be integrating its taxonomy into our agent evaluation pipeline to harden our own document manipulation skills against these three failure modes.
