## Train the Model, Not the Reader: Decodability Supervision for Verifiable Activation Explanations

**Authors:** Hiskias Dingeto (single author) · **Paper:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20379

### The problem

Natural-language autoencoders ("verbalizers") claim to explain hidden activations by a simple test: if the activation can be reconstructed from the explanation, it's deemed faithful. Dingeto shows this test is structurally broken. Flipping a single claim in the explanation barely changes reconstruction quality — meaning ~98% of specific claims could be lies and the score wouldn't care. Under synthetic ground-truth experiments, the standard training recipe develops "co-adapted private codes" — false wording that the reconstruction happens to depend on — in 5 out of 5 runs. The test tracks gist, not truth.

### The approach: RECAP

Rather than trying to audit explanations post-hoc (which the paper shows is inherently fragile), Dingeto proposes RECAP: **R**eadable **E**ncodings via **C**o-trained **A**uxiliary **P**redictors. Linear probe heads are trained alongside the target model to keep designated internal content independently decodable. This changes the game: instead of asking a model "write an explanation, then we'll check if it reconstructs" (which is gameable), RECAP makes the designated content directly checkable by independent probes. The cost is negligible (a +0.001 nat overhead).

### Why it matters

For interpretability, this is a quiet bomb: the dominant paradigm for scoring explanations (reconstruction fidelity) does not certify individual claims — it only tracks gist. For AI safety, the implications are sharper: RECAP's probes flag lies even when an adversary explicitly edits an explanation to maximize reconstruction score while lying (AUC 0.95 vs chance 0.51 for the control). The title says it all — train the *model* to be decodable, not the *reader* to be trusting. This reframes the entire interpretability-as-audit pipeline toward built-in verifiability rather than after-the-fact explanation scoring.
