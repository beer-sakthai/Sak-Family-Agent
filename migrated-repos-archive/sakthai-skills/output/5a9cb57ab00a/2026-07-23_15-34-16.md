**Paper:** *Generalizable VLA Finetuning via Representation Anchoring and Language-Action Alignment*
**arXiv:** [2607.13429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13429)
**Authors:** Dwip Dalal, Shivansh Patel, Chahit Jain, Jeonghwan Kim, Utkarsh Mishra, Alex Baratian, Hyeonjeong Ha, Heng Ji, Svetlana Lazebnik, Unnat Jain

---

The standard recipe for building vision-language-action (VLA) robot policies — finetune a pretrained VLM on robot demonstrations via behavior cloning — has a silent flaw. Every BC gradient step overwrites the pretrained representations that give the model its visual and semantic generalization. Co-training on web image-text data doesn't fix it because language and action losses land on *different* observations, never forcing the model to produce aligned representations from the same input. The authors name this **language-action misalignment** and show that standard manipulation benchmarks are too forgiving to expose it.

Their fix is **Anchor-Align**, two objectives layered on top of BC:

1. **Vision-Language Anchoring** — a layer-wise distillation loss from a frozen copy of the original VLM. This acts as a **representation brake**, preventing the finetuned model from drifting too far from the pretrained feature space that generalises well.
2. **Language-Action Alignment** — action targets are converted into discrete motion-direction labels (e.g. "move gripper down 3 cm"), and the model jointly predicts both the language label and the continuous action from the *same robot observation*. This forces shared representations for linguistic and motor understanding.

The results are concrete and cross-validated. On a physical xArm7 across two VLA architectures (Octo-based and RT-2-X-based), Anchor-Align lifts success rates from 28% → 54% and 37% → 60%, respectively. In simulation, it produces consistent gains on OOD visual perturbations (LIBERO-PRO), perceptual robustness (LIBERO-Plus), and long-horizon task completion (CALVIN). The paper's key insight is that preserving pretrained representations and learning effective actions are not a trade-off — you can have both, you just need the right auxiliary losses. For the robotics community racing to deploy VLAs on real hardware, this is a practical, implementation-ready improvement that costs almost nothing to add.
