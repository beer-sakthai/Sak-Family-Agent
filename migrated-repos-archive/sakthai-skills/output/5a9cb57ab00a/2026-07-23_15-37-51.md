## An Exam for Active Observers

**arXiv:** 2607.16165 · **HF:** https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.16165 · **Authors:** Zhang, Tao, Wang, Liu, Ma, Neiswanger (USC + CMU)

**The core problem.** Human vision is not a single snapshot — it's a closed loop where gaze is continuously redirected by intermediate hypotheses. This "active observation" is essential for many visual tasks. Yet every existing vision-language benchmark evaluates models on static descriptions: look once, answer. The question of whether today's multimodal LLMs can *actively observe* — re-examine, redirect attention, iteratively gather visual evidence — has never been tested. The authors close this gap with **ActiveVision**, a benchmark of 17 tasks across 3 categories designed specifically to force repeated visual perception rather than one-shot recognition.

**What they found is devastating.** GPT-5.5 at its highest reasoning-effort tier solves only **10.6%** of ActiveVision items — and scores zero on 11 of the 17 tasks. Claude Fable 5, which tops most reasoning and coding leaderboards, solves just **3.5%**. Three human participants average **96.1%**. Even when models are allowed to write and run their own vision code (e.g., crop, zoom, edge-detect), the gap barely narrows: such code is unreliable on realistic imagery, and catching its failures *itself* requires the active perception the models lack. No frontier model scored above 11%.

**Why it matters.** This isn't another incremental benchmark — it reveals a *structural blind spot* in the current paradigm. Scale, chain-of-thought, and agentic tool-use all assume the model can iteratively gather information. If the model cannot actively examine a visual scene — cannot decide *where to look next* based on what it just saw — then every downstream capability that depends on visual grounding (robotics, GUI agents, autonomous driving, scientific figure analysis) is bottlenecked by this missing primitive. The paper's message is unambiguous: current architectures have not closed the perception-reasoning loop, and closing it likely demands new training objectives, not just larger models or more data.
