## G-MAD: A Game-Based Data Generation Framework for Multi-View RGB-T Aerial Object Detection

**Paper:** [2607.19942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19942) | **Authors:** Kim, Park, Yoon, Jung, Jeon | **Date:** 2026-07-22

**The problem.** Aerial object detection in RGB-thermal (RGB-T) data suffers from a stubborn data bottleneck. Real-world multi-view aerial datasets are brutally expensive to collect: you need coordinated drone flights, near-perfect visible/thermal sensor alignment, diverse weather/lighting conditions, and armies of annotators. This means existing benchmarks are small, narrow in viewpoint coverage, and often poorly aligned between modalities — making it nearly impossible to study how viewpoint variation, multi-modal fusion strategy, and domain gap actually affect detection performance.

**The approach.** G-MAD turns the problem on its head by generating synthetic training data from a video game engine — Arma3, chosen for its realistic terrain, lighting, and vehicle models. The framework gives researchers structured control over scenarios (urban, rural, maritime, etc.), multi-camera rig placement (arbitrary number of RGB+thermal pairs at any angle/distance), synchronized capture of both modalities, and automatic ground-truth bounding boxes extracted directly from engine-level geometry metadata — zero human annotation. Using this pipeline the authors construct and release **AMOD** (Aerial Multi-view Object Detection), a large-scale benchmark designed to support controlled studies of the synthetic-to-real transfer gap.

**Key results and why it matters.** G-MAD's contribution isn't a new detection architecture — it's an infrastructure-level unlock. By eliminating the annotation bottleneck and providing perfect RGB-T alignment with known camera poses, the framework enables apples-to-apples comparisons of fusion strategies (early, mid, late), systematic measurement of how detection degrades with off-nadir viewing angles, and controlled simulation of the real-world domain gap. For the broader community, this represents a template: if you can simulate the sensor physics in a game engine, you can generate arbitrarily large training sets for tasks where real data is scarce. Expect G-MAD-style game-to-real pipelines to proliferate across remote sensing, autonomous driving, and robotics — anywhere multi-modal aerial data is too expensive to collect at scale.
