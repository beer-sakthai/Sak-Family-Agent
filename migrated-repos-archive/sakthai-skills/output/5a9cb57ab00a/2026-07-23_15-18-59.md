**Paper:** SLPO: Scaling Latent Reasoning via a Surrogate Policy
**Authors:** You, Liu, Li, Li (multiple institutions)
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19691

---

**The problem.** Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards has been the recipe that unlocked test-time scaling for explicit Chain-of-Thought reasoners (think o1-style models). But this path is computationally expensive — every intermediate reasoning step must be decoded as a full language token. Latent reasoners, which carry intermediate computation as continuous vectors rather than discrete tokens, already match or surpass explicit CoT at much shorter horizons. Yet they remain stuck in imitation learning: they can only learn from demonstration, not from outcome rewards. The reason is that latent trajectories lack a tractable per-step likelihood and have no natural stopping interface under fixed thinking budgets, so outcome-reward RL (the thing that made CoT scale) simply cannot be applied.

**The approach.** SLPO — Surrogate Latent Policy Optimization — bridges this gap with two key innovations. First, it introduces an *empirical surrogate policy density* over latent transitions, enabling trajectory-level credit assignment (i.e., figuring out which latent steps helped and which didn't) despite the intractable true likelihood. Second, it adds a *correctness-supervised stopping head* that learns to emit a stop signal calibrated to problem difficulty — optimized by the same outcome reward that drives the main policy. This turns fixed-budget latent reasoners into variable-horizon reasoners that allocate more computation to harder problems.

**Key results and why it matters.** Across both continuous and soft-thinking settings, SLPO significantly improves Pass@k under parallel sampling and, crucially, learns to allocate longer latent computation to harder instances — a behaviour that emerges purely from outcome-reward pressure, with higher deterministic accuracy as a result. This matters because it unblocks the primary scaling mechanism (outcome-reward RL) for the cheaper, faster latent reasoning paradigm. If latent reasoning can now scale with the same RL training dynamics that propelled explicit CoT, we may see reasoning models that are simultaneously *cheaper per token* and *trainable to superhuman performance* — the best of both worlds. SLPO is the missing piece that lets latent reasoners graduate from imitation to true RL-driven scaling.
