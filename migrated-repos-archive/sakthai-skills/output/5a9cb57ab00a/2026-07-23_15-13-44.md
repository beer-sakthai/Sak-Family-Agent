## ABot-World-0: Infinite Interactive World Rollout on a Single Desktop GPU

**Authors:** Fan Jiang, Zhaoxu Sun, Mengchao Wang et al. (41 authors)
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19191
**Vibe:** First action-conditioned world model to run real-time on a single consumer GPU — 199 ⭐ on HF today.

### The Problem

World models have been a research curiosity trapped in the cluster. They learn controllable environment dynamics from video, but have never been deployable — needing multi-GPU inference, seconds per frame, and dying from autoregressive drift after a few steps. Interactive use was a pipe dream.

### The Approach

ABot-World-0 tackles every bottleneck at once. On the **data side**, they built a multi-source pipeline spanning AAA games, physics simulators, and internet video — with **WorldExplorer** (agent-driven data collection guided by training feedback) and a 14-check deterministic quality pipeline plus VLM-based assessment. On the **model side**, they progressively distill a bidirectional action-conditioned teacher into a causal student using teacher forcing and ODE distillation, then introduce **LongForcing** — aligning long student self-rollouts with an extended-horizon teacher to suppress the drift that kills every other autoregressive world model. On the **deployment side**, they co-designed a streaming inference stack with a lightweight VAE decoder, efficient attention kernels, memory-aware scheduling, and low-bit DiT inference — all running raw keyboard actions in, frames out.

### Key Results & Why It Matters

**16 FPS at 720P on a single RTX 5090**, with 1.2s action-to-first-frame latency and ~19 GiB peak VRAM. This is the "Stable Diffusion moment" for interactive world models — the capability crossed the deployment threshold. Not only does it sustain coherent long-horizon rollouts (tested on the new WorldRoamBench and extended interactive sessions with third-person character control), but it runs on *the same GPU you game on*. The paper is as much an infrastructure contribution as a model contribution — the data pipeline, the distillation strategy, and the streaming inference stack together form a blueprint that other labs can replicate. World models just went from cluster-only research artifact to something you could ship as a product.
