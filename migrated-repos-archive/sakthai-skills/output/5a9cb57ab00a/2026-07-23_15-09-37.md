## Scaling Laws for Hypernetwork-Based Knowledge Injection in Large Language Models

**arXiv: 2607.19604** — July 2026

Injecting factual knowledge into LLMs at scale has remained a stubborn open problem — finetuning is expensive, catastrophic forgetting looms, and LoRA adapters struggle with multi-hop reasoning over thousands of facts. This paper asks a deceptively simple question: *can a hypernetwork, trained once on a large corpus, generate a single fixed LoRA adapter that makes a target model answer questions about those facts?* And more importantly, *does this ability follow predictable scaling laws?*

The answer is a clear yes on both counts. The authors build **MegaWikiQA**, a massive dataset of tens of millions of multi-hop QA examples across 39 domains derived from Wikidata5M. They then train hypernetworks at varying depths, widths, and target-model sizes to generate LoRA adapters for DeepSeek-scale base models. The core design insight is that decoupling the hypernetwork's injection capacity from the target model's general capability enables, for the first time, a rigorous scaling-law study of hypernetwork architectures themselves — not just the base LLM.

Results are striking: hypernetwork-based injection exhibits **broadly predictive power-law scaling** along all architectural axes (depth, width, target size). Loss, reasoning accuracy, and out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization all follow clean power laws. Crucially, hypernetworks show **steeper scaling exponents in OOD evaluations** compared to both standard LoRA finetuning and full finetuning — meaning at scale, hypernetworks *increasingly* outperform traditional adaptation methods on unseen fact compositions. This work establishes hypernetworks as a principled, scalable substrate for train-time knowledge injection and provides the first empirically grounded scaling laws to guide practitioners building factual reasoning into large models.
