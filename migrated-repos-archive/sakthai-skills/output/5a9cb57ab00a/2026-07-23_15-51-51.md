## SLAM in Low-Light Environments: Project Report

**Paper:** 2607.17699 · UCU Autonomous UGV Club · 4 authors · Submitted by YaroslavPrytula

**The problem everyone else skips.** SLAM benchmarks are shot in well-lit labs and sunlit streets — environments that flatter RGB cameras. Real-world robotics doesn't cooperate. Warehouses at night, underground tunnels, storm-damaged buildings: those are the conditions where SLAM actually needs to work, and where nearly every pipeline falls apart. Reduced contrast, sensor noise, and motion blur under 1 lux don't just degrade feature extraction — they break it entirely. This paper is a systematic stress test of how far RGB-only SLAM can be pushed when the lights go out.

**Six systems, five sequences, one survivor.** The authors benchmark six SLAM pipelines spanning every major paradigm — ORB-SLAM3 (feature-based), DSO (direct), Kimera-VIO (tightly-coupled VIO with factor-graph optimization), OpenVINS (filter-based MSCKF), DPVO (learning-based), and DPV-SLAM (learning-based with mapping) — on the LaMARia dataset across five sequences graded by difficulty and illumination. The results are stark: Kimera-VIO is the *only* system that completes all five sequences without losing tracking, achieving the lowest relative pose error. But even Kimera-VIO accumulates absolute drift (~tens of meters) because it lacks loop closure in its configuration. The learning-based methods (DPVO, DPV-SLAM) never lose tracking either, but their absolute error balloons to roughly 100 meters under low light — stable, but inaccurate. Every classical monocular pipeline (ORB-SLAM3, DSO) and the filter-based OpenVINS fails or diverges on most of the hard and low-light sequences.

**What the result means for real deployment.** Two ingredients proved necessary for low-light survival: inertial fusion (IMU) plus global optimization (factor graphs). Neither alone sufficed — OpenVINS has IMU but no global optimization, and it failed. DPV-SLAM has neither and tracked but at 100 m error. The takeaway for anyone building a field-deployable robot today: if you're running RGB-only SLAM in the dark without both IMU and a back-end optimizer, you're flying blind. The paper's final note — that the remaining gap probably needs learned low-light front-ends purpose-trained on degraded imagery rather than yet another filtering architecture — points toward where the next generation of dark-operable SLAM will come from.
