## FVAttn: Adaptive Sparse Attention with Runtime Load Balancing for Video Generation

**Authors:** Hao Liu, Chenghuan Huang, Ye Huang, Zhiying Wen, Hao Liu, Mohan Zhang, Chen Li, Ziyang Ma, Jing Lyu, Jiangsu Du
**arXiv:** [2607.16190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16190) | **HF Papers:** [https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.16190](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.16190)

**The problem.** Video Diffusion Transformers process long spatio-temporal sequences, making self-attention the main throughput bottleneck — especially at high resolutions. Training-free sparse attention (routing each query to only its Top-*p* most relevant keys) cuts the compute, but it creates a hidden distributed nightmare: adaptive Top-*p* routing produces *uneven per-head workloads* under multi-GPU sequence parallelism. Some ranks finish in a flash; others lag far behind. What was meant to be a speedup becomes a rank-level straggler problem that wastes the very parallelism you bought.

**The approach.** FVAttn is a training-free sparse-attention system that keeps the model untouched and instead fixes the *runtime execution*. It builds on Top-*p* routing with a Top-*k* safety floor and video-aware block organization, then repairs the attention mask at runtime. Three innovations make it work: (1) **Runtime Load Balancing** — migrates a small number of heavy heads via P2P communication to shorten the current critical path; (2) **Slack-Aware Sparse Augmentation** — fills residual non-critical-rank slack with additional high-value blocks rather than leaving GPU cycles idle; and (3) **Overlap** — hides scheduling and migration overhead behind existing computation.

**Key results.** On step-distilled Wan2.2 I2V, FVAttn reduces average load imbalance from 1.34 → 1.08, delivers a **4.41× attention speedup over FlashAttention**, and achieves **2.02–2.11× end-to-end DiT inference speedup** while maintaining competitive video quality. The speedups come entirely from runtime engineering — no retraining, no distillation, no model surgery.

**Why it matters.** This is the first work to diagnose and solve the distributed-execution inefficiency of adaptive sparse attention for video DiTs. As video generation models scale to higher resolutions and longer durations, attention becomes the dominant cost — and multi-GPU parallelism is the only escape. FVAttn shows that the next frontier of video generation speedup isn't better architectures or smaller models; it's smarter runtime scheduling that makes sparse attention actually *fast in practice*. The 2× free speedup means higher-resolution, longer-duration video generation becomes viable on existing GPU clusters without any model changes.
