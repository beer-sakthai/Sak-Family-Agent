## Beyond Relevance-Centric Retrieval: Rubric-Oriented Document Set Selection and Ranking

**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.19747 · Submitted 22 Jul 2026
**Authors:** Jiang, Liu, Xi, Xu, Liu, Fu, Ren, Li, Vichwang, Lu, Shi

**The problem.** Every RAG pipeline, search engine, and AI agent retrieves documents one at a time — score each doc against the query, rank by score, take the top-K. This "pointwise relevance" paradigm has ruled IR for decades. But when LLMs consume the results, the quality of the *set* matters, not just individual scores. Two documents that each score 0.9 may be redundant; a 0.7 doc that covers a missing facet beats a second 0.9 that repeats the first. Existing metrics like nDCG can't capture inter-document interactions (redundancy, conflict, complementarity), meaning the retrieval layer silently caps downstream generation quality.

**The approach.** The authors propose a complete evaluate-diagnose-optimize framework. First, **SetwiseEvalKit** — a benchmark of ~28K rubric-annotated document sets spanning short-form (multi-document QA) and long-form (report generation) scenarios, scored across 3 levels and 9 dimensions (coverage, novelty, coherence, non-redundancy, etc.). They evaluate 12 rerankers against it and find: even the best method achieves ≤45% rubric coverage, cross-document coordination dimensions are universally weak, and no single reranker dominates both short-form and long-form. Then **Rubric4Setwise** — a training-free method that converts rubric-based evaluation criteria into document set selection signals. Instead of ranking docs independently, it selects documents to collectively satisfy a multi-dimensional rubric.

**Key results & why it matters.** Rubric4Setwise achieves the best downstream generation performance with *fewer* documents and *fewer* search rounds than any pointwise baseline. It is the only method that maintains state-of-the-art across both short-form and long-form scenarios. This is a paradigm shift: the authors prove that evaluating retrieval as a *set selection problem* (rather than pointwise relevance scoring) unlocks a quality ceiling that pointwise methods cannot breach. For anyone building RAG systems, AI agents that consume search results, or next-generation search engines, this paper provides both the diagnostic framework to find your retrieval bottleneck and the optimizer to fix it — without additional training or model fine-tuning.
