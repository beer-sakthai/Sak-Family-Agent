## Trace: A Taxonomy-Guided Environment for Multidomain Visual Reasoning

**Paper:** https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.19790
**Authors:** Md Tanvirul Alam
**Category:** cs.CV (Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition)
**Date:** 22 Jul 2026

---

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has transformed language-model reasoning — it's what gave us the post-training leaps in DeepSeek-R1, OpenAI o1, and Qwen's reasoning models. But extending RLVR to **vision-language models** (VLMs) has been frustratingly slow. The bottleneck isn't the algorithm; it's the training data. Existing visual reasoning datasets are either too narrow (one domain, one question template) or too opaque (answers generated by a black-box VLM grader with no ground-truth guarantee). Trace attacks this problem head-on.

Trace is a **taxonomy-guided procedural environment** that factorizes visual reasoning task construction into two decoupled components: a **scene grammar** that generates the rendered image, and an **executable task program** that computes the answer. A shared semantic state ties everything together — image, prompt, typed answer, verifier, and a replayable trace — so every training instance is exactly verifiable and fully reproducible. The result is 1,000 task types spanning 277 scene grammars across 11 visual domains (geometry, counting, spatial relations, color, etc.), each with controlled semantic and visual variation.

The results are compelling: RLVR training on 64,000 Trace instances boosted macro-average accuracy across **24 external benchmarks** by 3.51 percentage points for Qwen2.5-VL-3B and 4.06 points for Qwen2.5-VL-7B. That's significant — these gains transfer well beyond Trace's own task distribution, proving that broad procedural training data can generalize. For a field where synthetic data is often accused of teaching narrow distributional tricks, Trace provides real evidence to the contrary. If this approach scales (and the architecture is designed to), it could become the standard recipe for post-training VLMs: generate a broad procedural curriculum with exact verifiers, then let RLVR do the rest.
