**Moving Alphabet: A Controlled Study of Training Data for Text-to-Video Generation** — Zheng, Liu, Yeh, Yin (arXiv 2607.18789)

Text-to-video has exploded over the last five years through scaling model size, data, and compute — but while architectures get all the attention, the *data* side remains surprisingly under-explored. Real-world video data curation is a messy pipeline: clip selection from raw footage, caption generation, filtering, deduplication. Each step injects biases, and we know remarkably little about how those biases affect downstream model behavior. This paper builds a clean experimental setup to answer exactly that question.

The authors introduce **Moving Alphabet**, a fully procedural testbed that renders letters on a black background with controlled variations in font, color, size, position, direction, and speed — yielding perfect ground-truth metadata. This lets them corrupt the data distribution and caption quality in precise, measurable ways, isolating causal effects that would be impossible to study in messy real-world datasets. The setup is elegant: because they *know* the ground truth, they can degrade it systematically and watch what breaks.

Three results stand out. **First**, a diverse and balanced distribution of video content *and* duration is critical for generalization — skewed data produces models that fail on unseen combinations. **Second**, caption quality dramatically affects both final performance *and* training efficiency, suggesting that T2V models are fundamentally bottlenecked by video understanding capabilities rather than generation capacity. **Third**, classifier-free guidance and fine-tuning on high-quality data can *partially* recover from corrupted pre-training data, but cannot fully compensate — garbage-in fundamentally limits what fine-tuning can fix. The implication is clear: the field needs to invest far more in the *science* of pre-training data curation, not just model scaling.
