## SLAI T-Rex: Full-Parameter Post-training of the DeepSeek-V4 Family on Ascend SuperPOD

**Paper:** [2607.20145](https://huggingface.co/papers/2607.20145) · 65 authors · 19 upvotes

**The problem.** Full-parameter post-training of a trillion-parameter MoE model is a systems engineering nightmare even on well-trodden NVIDIA GPU clusters — memory pressure, non-overlapped communication, and inefficient kernel execution compound at that scale. Doing it on an Ascend NPU SuperPOD, where the software ecosystem and kernel libraries are far less mature than CUDA's, is a qualitatively harder challenge. Prior work hadn't demonstrated that the DeepSeek-V4 family (with its Mixture-of-Experts architecture) could be post-trained end-to-end on non-NVIDIA hardware at all, let alone with competitive efficiency.

**What they did.** The team built SLAI T-Rex, a hierarchical optimisation framework that attacks the problem at three levels: model-level parallelism (sharding the 671B-param MoE across NPUs), computation-communication orchestration (overlapping all-gather/reduce with forward/backward compute to hide latency), and low-level kernel execution (fused operators custom-tuned for the Ascend CANN runtime). The result is a measured 34.22% Model FLOPs Utilization — a 2.93× improvement over the open-source baseline recipe — while maintaining training stability across the full post-training run. On top of that infra, they built a Continual Pre-Training and Supervised Fine-Tuning pipeline specifically for Operations Research (OR) tasks: 10K high-quality SFT samples covering four task categories and three problem representations, combining domain resources with solver-verified synthetic optimisation documents.

**Why it matters.** This is the first end-to-end proof that trillion-parameter frontier models can be post-trained on Ascend hardware at competitive efficiency. The OR-specialised DeepSeek-V4-Flash variant achieves 71.81% zero-shot Pass@1, beating GPT-5.4-Mini by 3.98 percentage points and the base DeepSeek-V4-Flash by 11.27 points — on complex solver-grounded mathematical reasoning, not just language modelling. The implications are twofold: first, the Chinese AI hardware ecosystem (Ascend NPUs) is now demonstrably viable for frontier-model post-training, reducing NVIDIA lock-in; second, domain-specialised post-training on efficient hardware yields results that outperform general-purpose frontier APIs on specific reasoning domains, reinforcing the case for fine-tuning over API-calling for production workloads.
